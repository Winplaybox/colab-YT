## ⚖️ Part 3: Handling Textual Variations & Narrative Etiquette
Hindu literature is characterized by rich multi-millennial traditions (Puranic, Itihasic, Agamic, and regional). To maintain the highest integrity and trust:

1. **Explicit Text Attribution**:
   - Use phrasing: *"According to the Valmiki Ramayana..."*, *"In the Shiva Purana tradition..."*, *"As recorded in the Bhagavata Purana..."*.
2. **Respecting Regional & Folk Variations**:
   - If a popular story differs from the primary Sanskrit text (e.g. Lakshman Rekha, which appears in *Ramcharitmanas* and *Krittibasi Ramayan* but not explicitly in *Valmiki Ramayana*), explicitly state:
     > *"While popular regional traditions recount the Lakshman Rekha, the early Sanskrit Valmiki Ramayana describes the event through Sita's direct dialogue..."*
3. **No Unverified Modern Serial Tropes**:
   - Modern television dramas often invent romantic drama, fictional villains, or anachronistic dialogue. All such tropes are strictly filtered out in favor of the Sanskrit text.

- **Accession Highlights**:
  - Durga Mahishasuramardini stone steles & bronzes
  - Folios of the *Harivamsa* and *Rasikapriya*

- **Allowed**: `CC0`, `Public Domain (PD-old-100)`, `PD-Art`, `CC-BY-4.0` (with onscreen creator credit).
- **Disallowed**: Unverified modern web images, copyrighted stock photos, non-commercial only (`CC-BY-NC`) photos.
